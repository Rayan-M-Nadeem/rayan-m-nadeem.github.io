From a neighborhood beginning

Incorporated on December 12, 1974, the Club first operated at 508-A Charles Street with support from the Easton Housing Authority. Boys and girls participated from the beginning.

Growing opportunities

Future Stars began in 1984. The first Easton Jazz Festival followed in 1987. The Club formally adopted the Boys & Girls Club of Easton name in 1991. Performing arts, prevention and teen leadership expanded through the 1990 s; the Martin Luther King, Jr. Community Center was dedicated in 1997.

Learning and community

The golf fundraiser began in 2000. Following relocation during housing redevelopment in 2008, the Club reopened after remodeling in 2011. Its timeline records Istation in 2014, the EASD 21st Century partnership in 2016, DreamBox in 2017, Lexia in 2018, a playground in 2019 and a community garden in 2020.

More of our story

The club began as a neighborhood place for boys and girls on Charles Street in 1974. Over the years, its work has grown to include academic support, arts, sports, healthy habits, leadership, and community events.

Its purpose is to help young people, especially those who need support most, develop their abilities and contribute to their community. Families can start by visiting the club and meeting the people behind its programs.

The original website records milestones from Future Stars and the Easton Jazz Festival to learning partnerships, a playground, and a community garden. Explore the history page for the story behind the club.
