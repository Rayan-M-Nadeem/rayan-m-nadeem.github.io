Our mission and purpose

EACC provides a safe setting, programs, resources, and role models that support the social, physical, and mental well-being of young people and adults. Its vision centers on community development and well-being.

Children ages 5–18 can take part in educational and athletic activities, after-school enrichment, teen mentoring, and summer camp. Adult programs are also offered. The center provides supervised afternoons, homework support, life skills, and positive alternatives to unsafe activities.

The previous site describes a neighborhood of 11,500 residents, including 1,000 children and young people, and a service history spanning more than six decades. Those figures are retained as historical context.

More of our story

Support that goes beyond an activity EACC’s mission connects education and recreation with the social, physical, and emotional well-being of young people and adults. Homework support, positive role models, and opportunities to build life skills are part of the center’s purpose.

People who know the community The staff page introduces Maria Laboy and Mayche Webster as senior-program coordinators, Derek Glenn as food director, and Judy Dickerson as the leader of the Cops ’n’ Kids reading center. Their work brings together activities, meals, literacy, and relationships across generations.

Learning with local partners Lafayette College documented student volunteers mentoring children at EACC during fall 2023. Alongside tutoring, the students joined games, creative projects, and wellness activities such as yoga and breathing exercises. That account shows how a community partnership can add individual attention to a child’s time at the center.

EACC is an independent nonprofit serving Easton’s West Ward. Its work starts with a safe, supportive place for young people, with adults who help them learn, build confidence, and find their interests.

After school, that means homework help, art, music, reading, and time in the gym. Summer camp keeps children learning and active beyond the school year. The Saints sports programs give young athletes a team to belong to.

The center is here for adults, too. Silver Connections brings older neighbors together for activities and meals. English classes, food distribution, and community partnerships add practical support for local families.

The older name is part of the story The center’s 60th-anniversary coverage traces St. Anthony’s Youth Center to a church basement in 1953 and a move to the present location in 1958. That is why longtime residents may know the same organization by a different name.

A broader community role In a 2011 presentation to County Council, the center’s president explained that the 2004 name change reflected service to the wider community and a broader range of programs. The later reading-room and college-mentoring partnerships add further chapters to that story. Today’s program pages show educational, athletic, social, and practical support sharing the same neighborhood home.

Milestones in the center’s history

  1. 1953

    St. Anthony’s Youth Center took shape in the basement of St. Anthony’s Church. The center celebrated this beginning at its 60th anniversary in 2013.

  2. 1958

    The youth center moved to its present home at Ninth and Washington streets, putting down roots in the West Ward.

  3. 2004

    The organization adopted the name Easton Area Community Center to reflect its work with the wider community and its expanding range of programs.

  4. 2011

    Easton’s Cops ’n’ Kids Reading Room opened on June 4, supported by the Police Athletic League, EACC, and other community partners. Books and shared reading became another way for families to connect.

  5. 2023

    Lafayette students joined children at EACC for tutoring, games, arts, and wellness activities through the Landis Center’s MOSAIC program, documented by the college in January 2024.
