Follow the water. Read the evidence.

Local residents monitor the creek each month. The official program describes 26 testing locations and publishes monthly maps and data sheets, preserving an accessible record over time.

The watershedProject archiveMonitoringDocument libraryGet involved

What volunteers measure

Testing uses LaMotte kits and includes nitrates, phosphates, alkalinity, pH, dissolved oxygen and conductivity. Weather, temperature, geology, runoff and tributary flows all influence results. A single reading should be understood in that context.

Published records, clearly dated

September 2026 is the latest monthly record linked on the official page when checked on October 19, 2026. Open its original map or data sheet, or browse the Conservancy’s full archive. These are dated observations, not a live water-safety assessment.

Stream flow from USGS

USGS also maintains a monitoring-location page for Bushkill Creek above the Route 33 bridge at Tatamy. Use the agency’s page for the available measurements and their timestamps.

Measurements need context

Nitrates, phosphates, alkalinity, pH, dissolved oxygen and conductivity describe different aspects of stream conditions. Weather, temperature, geology and runoff affect the observations. One result cannot stand in for the condition of the whole watershed.

The official archive still identified September 2026 as its latest published month when checked October 26, 2026. Use the original dated chart and map together, and follow the archive for later releases. No observations have been recreated or altered here.
