Florence and Venice in the Renaissance

Florence and Venice were wealthy, competitive cities whose art and learning grew within complex political and commercial systems.

01 / The story

Cities rather than a nation

The Italian peninsula contained republics, duchies, papal territories, and kingdoms. Florence’s Medici used banking, patronage, and political networks to wield power within republican forms. Venice’s maritime empire connected merchants to the eastern Mediterranean. Rivalry could stimulate innovation and war.

02 / The story

Art as work and power

Humanists studied classical texts, while artists developed approaches to perspective, anatomy, and proportion. Workshops, patrons, printers, and skilled craftspeople made these works possible. Celebrated masterpieces were also commissions that expressed religious devotion, family status, and political ambition.

03 / The story

Who is left outside the portrait

Women participated as patrons, workers, writers, and members of households, though their opportunities were constrained. Trade networks depended on labor beyond Italy and on unequal relationships. “Rebirth” is a label for certain changes, not a claim that earlier centuries had no culture.

The longer viewCultural achievement is inseparable from institutions, money, craft, and access.
