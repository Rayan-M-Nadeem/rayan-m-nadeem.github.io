A course of ideas

Start with structure. Follow the change.

These chapters follow the modern nine-unit introductory chemistry sequence, but the explanations are written for anyone curious.

01

Atoms & evidence

An atom is not a tiny solar system. Its identity is set by its protons, while its electrons determine most of its chemistry.

Atomic structure and properties ↗02

Bonds & structure

A chemical bond is an energy-favorable arrangement of electrons and nuclei, not a physical stick between atoms.

Compound structure and properties ↗03

Mixtures & forces

What matter does in bulk depends on how its particles attract, separate, and move.

Properties of substances and mixtures ↗04

Reactions & accounting

A reaction rearranges atoms and electrons. The equation is an accounting statement before it is a prediction.

Chemical reactions ↗05

Reaction rates

A reaction may be favorable and still take a long time. Kinetics asks how fast it happens and what path it takes.

Kinetics ↗06

Heat & enthalpy

Energy changes accompany reactions, but heat is energy crossing a boundary—not a substance stored inside a beaker.

Thermochemistry ↗07

Dynamic equilibrium

At equilibrium, forward and reverse changes keep occurring at equal rates. Nothing has to stop or become equal in amount.

Equilibrium ↗08

Acids, bases & buffers

Acid–base behavior is about proton transfer and competing equilibria, not simply whether a liquid feels “strong.”

Acids and bases ↗09

Free energy & cells

A reaction’s direction depends on an interplay of energy and dispersal; electron transfer can turn that tendency into electrical work.
