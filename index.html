What modern medicine can already do, and what it cannot do yet.

A student's field guide to the science that is quietly extending human life.

This site collects four connected topics in modern medicine: the heart, the brain, cancer, and the wider project of extending healthy human life. Each page is written for a general reader, but it takes the science seriously. The aim is neither the despair that says nothing can be done nor the hype that promises we will defeat death soon. It is the harder middle position, which is to show what the tools actually do today, where the limits are, and what would have to change for the next few decades to look different from the last.

About This Project

I am a high school student who writes independent research papers to understand large questions from the ground up. This project grew out of a survey I wrote on life-extending medicine, and the three shorter pages here are the pillars that survey rests on. Cardiovascular disease, neurodegeneration, and cancer are the failure modes that decide how long most people live.

I built this site to practice something medicine actually requires, which is explaining hard science to the people it affects without either frightening them or making it smaller than it is. If a page here makes a difficult idea clearer without misleading you, it has done its job.
