About this project

Plainly Medicine is an independent research project about what modern medicine can already accomplish—and where its limits still are. The site grew from a longer survey I wrote on life-extending medicine. These eleven pieces examine the systems and diseases that most strongly determine how long and how well people live, from cardiovascular disease and neurodegeneration to cancer, transplantation, and gene editing.

I built the project around a simple standard: explain difficult science without flattening it. Each piece is written for a general reader, but the evidence is not simplified away. Clinical claims are linked to primary sources, uncertainty is stated directly, and the distinction between established medicine and genuine frontier research is kept clear.

I am an independent student researcher, not a licensed medical professional. This site is educational and does not provide medical advice.
